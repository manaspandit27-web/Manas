# Bramblewood Ventures — Pitch Screening Dealbook (internal)

Bramblewood is an early-stage venture fund. Every inbound pitch gets one of three answers:

- `pass`: a polite no.
- `meeting`: a first call with an associate.
- `fast_track`: a partner meeting this week.

Every answer also carries a **reason code**, so founders get a consistent explanation and the partners can audit the funnel.

## How to screen a pitch

Work down the steps in order. **The first step that applies decides the answer**; stop there.

| Step | Check | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The round size is outside the range for its stage (Table A), or the stage is not pre-seed, seed or Series A | `pass` | `stage_or_size` |
| 2 | The headquarters is outside where we invest (Table B) | `pass` | `geography` |
| 3 | The sector is on the exclusion list (Table C) | `pass` | `excluded_sector` |
| 4 | The startup competes with an **active** portfolio company (Appendix) | `pass` | `portfolio_conflict` |
| 5 | The sector is not one of our thesis sectors (Table D) | `pass` | `outside_thesis` |
| 6a | The sector's must-have (Table D) is missing, but a founder has a qualifying exit | `meeting` | `repeat_founder` |
| 6b | The sector's must-have (Table D) is missing | `pass` | `thesis_requirement_missing` |
| 7 | A founder has a qualifying exit | `fast_track` | `repeat_founder_exit` |
| 8 | Traction is at least **3×** the meeting bar (Table E) | `fast_track` | `strong_traction` |
| 9 | Traction is at least the meeting bar (Table E) | `meeting` | `thesis_fit` |
| 10 | The pitch came through an intro from a Bramblewood portfolio founder | `meeting` | `portfolio_intro` |
| 11 | None of the above | `pass` | `insufficient_traction` |

### Definitions

- **Qualifying exit:** a founder previously co-founded a company that was acquired or went public at a value of **$50M or more**. A smaller sale, a shutdown, or having been an early employee does not count.
- **Competes:** the startup's main product serves the **same market** as a portfolio company's, as listed in the Appendix. A neighbouring market does not count: restaurant scheduling software does not compete with restaurant payroll software.
- **Active vs exited:** only *active* portfolio companies block a pitch. Exited ones are listed so you know they do not.
- **Portfolio intro:** only an intro from a founder of a Bramblewood portfolio company, active or exited, counts. Intros from other investors, advisors or our own LPs do not.
- **Headquarters** is where the company is incorporated and run, not where the founders are from.
- **Business model** (for Table E) is how the startup makes most of its money today.

## Table A — Stage and round size

| Stage | Round size we consider |
|---|---|
| Pre-seed | $0.5M – $2.5M |
| Seed | $2M – $6M |
| Series A | $6M – $20M |

A round outside the range for its own stage is a pass, even if the amount would fit another stage. A Series B or later, or a grant-only raise, is also a pass.

## Table B — Where we invest

| Headquarters | Stages |
|---|---|
| United States, Canada | Pre-seed, seed, Series A |
| United Kingdom, Ireland | Series A only |
| Anywhere else | None |

## Table C — Excluded sectors

We never invest in: gambling and sports betting · tobacco and vaping · cannabis · weapons · crypto tokens and token-funded projects · payday and high-interest consumer lending · adult content · fossil-fuel extraction.

## Table D — Thesis sectors and their must-haves

| Thesis sector | Must-have |
|---|---|
| Vertical software (software for one industry: restaurants, construction, dental, legal, trucking, …) | A founder worked **5+ years** in the industry they sell to |
| Fintech infrastructure | A signed **partner bank**, or the company holds its **own licence** (money transmission, lending) |
| Insurtech | A signed **licensed carrier or MGA** partner |
| Digital health | A **clinician (MD, DO, NP or RN)** on the founding team or board |
| Medical devices | A **510(k) or De Novo** regulatory pathway. Devices that need a PMA fail this |
| Climate hardware | A **paid pilot** with an industrial or utility customer |
| Energy and grid software | At least one **utility or grid-operator** customer, paid or pilot |
| Developer tools | An open-source project with **1,000+ GitHub stars**, or **10+ paying company customers** |
| AI applications | A **proprietary or exclusive data source** the product is built on |
| AI infrastructure | **3+ paying enterprise design partners** |
| Cybersecurity | A founder with **3+ years on a security team**, in a company or in government |
| Education | Sells to **institutions** (schools, districts, universities, employers). Selling direct to learners fails this |
| Supply chain and logistics | At least one customer with **$100M+ in annual revenue** |
| Robotics and automation | Units **deployed at a paying customer** |
| Food and agriculture tech | A signed **retail or distribution agreement** |
| HR and workforce | Sells to **employers**. Selling direct to individual workers fails this |

Sectors not on this list are outside our thesis (step 5) even when they are good businesses, for example: drug discovery and therapeutics · consumer social apps · games · direct-to-consumer brands · media and content · restaurants and physical retail · space · semiconductors.

## Table E — Traction: the meeting bar

| Business model | Traction metric | Seed | Series A |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B software (subscription or usage-based) | Annual recurring revenue | $150k | $1M |
| Marketplace | Monthly gross transaction volume | $100k | $750k |
| Consumer subscription | Paying subscribers | 5,000 | 40,000 |
| Hardware and robotics | Signed contracts plus paid pilots, total value | $250k | $2M |

- The **fast-track bar** (step 8) is 3× the meeting bar: for example $450k ARR at seed, or $3M at Series A.
- **Pre-seed has no meeting bar.** A pre-seed pitch that reaches step 8 cannot fast-track on traction, and always clears step 9.

## Worked examples

**1.** *"Brineworks, Austin. Raising a $3.5M seed. Inventory software for independent butcher shops; our CEO ran her family's shop for nine years. $210k ARR."*
Steps 1–4 clear (seed range, US, not excluded, no portfolio company in butcher-shop inventory). Step 5: vertical software is a thesis sector. Step 6: nine years in the industry meets the must-have. Step 7: no prior exit. Step 8: $210k is below $450k. Step 9: $210k is above $150k. → `meeting`, `thesis_fit`.

**2.** *"Toronto, raising a $12M Series A. AI scribe for veterinary clinics, trained on 4M exclusive visit records. $2.4M ARR."*
Step 4: Scribeleaf (active) does AI note-taking for veterinary clinics. → `pass`, `portfolio_conflict`. Strong traction does not matter; step 4 comes first.

**3.** *"London, raising a $1M pre-seed for an open-source API testing tool with 3,000 GitHub stars."*
Step 2: UK companies are considered at Series A only. → `pass`, `geography`.

**4.** *"Boston, raising a $4M seed for a new cardiac implant that will go through PMA. Our CEO co-founded a diagnostics company acquired for $120M."*
Steps 1–5 clear (medical devices is a thesis sector). Step 6a: a PMA device misses the must-have, but the CEO has a qualifying exit. → `meeting`, `repeat_founder`.

**5.** *"Chicago, raising a $2.5M seed. Accounts-payable software for restaurant groups; our founder was a restaurant CFO for 12 years. $80k ARR, introduced by the founder of Pennywhistle."*
Step 4: Pennywhistle is in this market but has exited, so no conflict. Steps 5–6: vertical software, must-have met. Steps 7–9: no exit, and $80k is below the $150k bar. Step 10: Pennywhistle's founder counts as a portfolio founder. → `meeting`, `portfolio_intro`.
